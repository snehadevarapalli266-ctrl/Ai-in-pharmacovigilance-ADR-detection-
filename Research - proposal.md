# Research Proposal

## Title
Evaluation of Artificial Intelligence-Assisted Adverse Drug Reaction Detection

## 1. Background
Pharmacovigilance supports the identification and assessment of
suspected adverse drug reactions. Artificial intelligence,
particularly natural language processing, may help extract
medicine and adverse-event information from large volumes of text.

## 2. Research Question
How accurately can an NLP-assisted method identify suspected
adverse drug reactions in a suitable public research dataset?

## 3. Hypothesis
An NLP-assisted method may identify relevant medicine and
adverse-event mentions, but its predictions will require
human verification.

## 4. Aim
To investigate published evidence on AI-assisted ADR detection
and propose a feasible evaluation of an NLP-based approach.

## 5. Objectives
- Review relevant scientific publications.
- Identify common approaches to AI-assisted ADR detection.
- Examine limitations reported in existing studies.
- Propose an evaluation using a suitable public dataset.
- Identify safeguards for human review and responsible data use.

## 6. Proposed Methodology
Study design: A retrospective methodological pilot.

Data source: A suitable publicly available, de-identified
dataset with reference annotations, subject to licence and
access conditions.

Procedure:
1. Select and document the dataset.
2. Choose a suitable baseline NLP method.
3. Compare its predictions with reference annotations.
4. Calculate precision, recall, and F1-score where appropriate.
5. Review common errors and document limitations.

No experiment will be considered completed until the
analysis has actually been performed.

## 7. Ethical Considerations
Only authorized data will be used. Confidential patient
information will not be uploaded to public repositories.
AI outputs will be treated as decision-support information,
not confirmed clinical findings.

## 8. Expected Outcomes
The proposed study may clarify the strengths and limitations
of NLP-assisted ADR detection and identify areas requiring
further validation.

## 9. Limitations
The findings may depend on dataset quality, annotation
methods, and the selected NLP approach. A small benchmark
cannot establish clinical effectiveness across all settings.

## 10. Conclusion
AI may support pharmacovigilance workflows, but reliable
validation, responsible data handling, and human expert
review remain essential.
